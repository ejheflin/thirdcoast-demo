The one match that is not a bracket

The grand final is the winners final's winner against the losers final's winner, so it belongs to neither tree — which is why both bracket screens hold one format all night and end in a to final arrow pointing here.

It is also the only screen showing a single match, so it can say what a bracket card cannot: how each side got here. In the captured tournament that is Notorious DIG at 5–0, undefeated through the winners bracket, against Beach Please at 3–2, who lost in Winners R2 and won three straight on the losers side to come back. That is the story of the night, and it is one line of arithmetic away from the data we already have.

Both states have to work — playing, and settled, which is the moment the whole board exists for. Grounds carry the same meaning as everywhere else: the finalist out of the winners bracket sits on winners blue, the one out of the losers bracket on losers pink, so anyone who has been watching the two screens already knows which is which.
