split brackets

A screen each

The winners tree gets a screen, the losers tree gets a screen, and the rotation shows them in turn. This is what the venue's own paper bracket does — two separate trees, cross-referenced by name rather than by line. Every winners match here carries a pink tag naming the losers slot its loser falls into, and every losers match carries that slot name. No connector is drawn between the two.

What splitting fixes

  1. A semi can sit between the two matches that feed it. Sharing a column put four losers matches beside two semis, so the winners lane got a third of the column while its feeders spread across all of it — a 417px centring error.
  2. Nothing crosses the middle of the tree. The dashed loser-drops are gone; a tag replaces them.
  3. A whole bracket fits. sizecheck says both brackets together stop fitting past 8 teams. One at a time, the whole winners tree fits at 12.

What it costs

Width. Each half is five rounds wide — winners R1, R2, semi, final and the grand final; losers R2 through the losers final — and five columns of card is a narrower card than three. The two variants are the two ways to spend that, and the captions carry the measurement that settles it: how many team names get clipped, counted off the rendered elements, worst case being "Save a Horse, Dig a Volleyball" at thirty characters.
