Tonight, on a tournament night

I had this wrong. I said the Tonight grid was meaningless on a playoff night because a team's next opponent is unknown. The unknown is the information — a team that just lost game one wants to see it is in the losers bracket, waiting on a specific game. The axis was wrong, not the grid. Slots become bracket rounds.

None of this is mocked. Teams come from the captured night, the bracket from domain.Generate, the state from domain.Replay over an event sequence — the same three functions the server uses. Every TBD below is a genuinely unresolved slot, and "Winner on Court 3" is read off the feeding match's live court rather than typed.

One measurement shapes all of these: a double-elim bracket has 8 rounds but each team plays only 2–3 of them, so a single flat round grid (P1) is 28% dense — much emptier than the game-night grid at 50%.

Pivoting the Winners/Losers label up onto a section header is what fixes that, and putting both lanes on one universal axis is what makes them readable together. R1 to R6 is shared, so Winners Semi and Losers R1 sit in the same column — they are the same round of the night. R1 has no losers cell, because at R1 nobody has lost yet.

That axis is not hand-assigned. It is each match's depth in the dependency graph, computed in Go from domain.Structure — so "the losers bracket has no R1" falls out of the maths rather than being asserted, and it stays correct for any bracket size or format.

A team sits in the lane of its most recent match — so a team that lost in winners appears in the losers lane, which is where it actually is. An empty lane is dropped entirely: nobody has reached the Grand Final yet, so that section does not render.
